The tax treatment of capital gains depends on how long you held the asset before selling it. If you held the asset for one year or less before selling it, the gain is considered a short-term capital gain. Short-term capital gains are taxed at your ordinary income tax rate, which is based on your tax bracket.If you held the asset for more than one year before selling it, the gain is considered a long-term capital gain. Long-term capital gains are taxed at a lower rate than short-term capital gains and ordinary income. The specific tax rate for long-term capital gains depends on your tax bracket.For example, for tax year 2021, if you are in the 24% tax bracket, your long-term capital gains tax rate is 15%. If you are in the 37% tax bracket, your long-term capital gains tax rate is 20%.It's important to note that these tax rates apply to the total amount of your capital gains for the year, not to each individual investment. So, if you have multiple investments that resulted in capital gains or losses during the year, you will need to calculate your net capital gain or loss for the year by adding up all of your capital gains and losses and then applying the appropriate tax rate.
