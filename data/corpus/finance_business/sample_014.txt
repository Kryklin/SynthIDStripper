Companies typically announce stock splits when the market price of their stock has risen significantly, and they want to make it more affordable for individual investors to purchase shares. A stock split involves increasing the number of outstanding shares of a company's stock, while simultaneously decreasing the price of each share. For example, if a company announces a 2-for-1 stock split, this means that each shareholder will receive an additional share of stock for every share they currently own, and the price of each share will be halved. The overall value of an individual's investment in the company remains the same after a stock split, but the lower price per share may make it more appealing to potential investors.Stock splits are usually announced by the company's board of directors, and are typically implemented through a stock dividend. After a stock split is announced, it typically takes a few weeks for the split to be completed, as the company's stock transfer agent needs to issue the additional shares and adjust the stock price.
